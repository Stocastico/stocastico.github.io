Multi-modal Speaker Diarisation at Broadcast Scale

Audio-only diarisation breaks down quickly in noisy multi-speaker broadcasts. Multi-modal fusion improves reliability, but system design is the hard part.

We combined speaker embeddings, lip-motion cues, and camera framing signals. The model stack worked well in isolation; operations and synchronization were the bigger challenge during real-time broadcast windows.

Key operational constraints

Stream alignment across modalities had to stay within strict tolerance. Small clock drifts created identity switches that were hard to debug after the event.

We prioritized graceful degradation: when video confidence dropped, the system shifted weight to audio while preserving temporal consistency.

Why this matters

Reliable diarisation powers downstream tasks like subtitle attribution, speaker analytics, and content indexing. Accuracy gains are valuable only if the pipeline can sustain live production load.
